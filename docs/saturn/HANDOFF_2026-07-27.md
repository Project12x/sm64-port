## The owner's redirect, verbatim intent

Incremental optimization of sourceboot's interpreted float pipeline produced
measured but **invisible** progress: 0.36 → 0.67 FPS across two days of
landed work. The owner's verdict: *"those measured wins are essentially
meaningless when a doubling in speed still is less than 1 fps"* — and they are
right. **Absolute FPS is the only metric from here. Stop reporting ratios.**

The new strategy is **demo-path first**: keep the real SM64 simulation as the
authority, but render through the fast baked-IR path this repo already owns,
accepting aggressive degradation on day one — **except textures, which are
explicitly in scope from the start.** The owner's correction, recorded so it
is not walked back: *castleviewer and the head demo had textures.* The demo
path must not regress to Gouraud-only.

Feasibility anchor, per the owner: **the Saturn ran Croc.** A textured 3-D
platformer at playable frame rates on this exact hardware, achieved with
disciplined view distances. Z-Treme and SlaveDriver budgets (~500–660
cycles/primitive) and our own castleviewer (~1,741) say the same thing. This
is not a research question; it is an integration question.

## Why this is credible: the fast renderer already exists

| Renderer, this repo | cycles/primitive | FPS (measured) | Textures |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `castleviewer` (m4) | ~1,741 | **14.93** | **Yes** — tile atlas, CLUT16, per-primitive Gouraud, residency |
| `sourceboot` today | ~35,000 | 0.67 | none |
| Shipping Saturn engines | ~500–660 | 30 (design) | yes |

Castleviewer renders **real SM64 source geometry** (castle interior, animated
source Mario) with **real textures** at ~15 FPS. The head demo (`introface`)
rendered the source Mario face with material colors and eye textures. The
speed and the textures are both proven *in this tree* — what has never been
done is putting the **real simulation** underneath them. That is the whole
remaining problem.

## The plan

### Step 0 — measure the sim tick (one day, decides everything)

Castleviewer's 14.93 FPS runs **no SM64 tick**. The simulation itself is
soft-float-heavy (`camera.c` alone: 382 call sites; `behaviors/*`: 1,218).
**Nobody has measured what one `game_loop_one_iteration()` costs in
isolation** — the phase timers three documents have requested still do not
exist. If the sim eats most of a 33 ms budget, that is the real wall and we
need to know *before* the renderer swap, not after.

Deliverable: a sim/render phase timer in the profile (append-only fields —
`fast3d_profile_decode.py` picks them up automatically), one route capture,
and the answer: **sim ms/tick at 30 Hz.** The FRT harness pattern exists at
`hwtest/main.c:163-190`.

### Step 1 — bake BOB into the IR, textures included

The offline pass that castleviewer already proved, pointed at BOB:

- Terrain geometry → compiled IR in work RAM (the cart is cold storage —
  Z-Treme's LWRAM→HWRAM hot promotion is **adoptable code** now, not a lesson).
- **Textures in the same bake**: the designed-and-committed texture spec
  (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-26-vdp1-textures-design.md`) already
  scopes exactly this — milestone 1 textures **1,077 of BOB's 1,101 terrain
  triangles** via per-primitive tile bake (16×16/32×32 CLUT16, 333,696 bytes
  against a measured 446,432 free VDP1 VRAM), keyed on
  `(display_list, list_ordinal)`. That spec was written for the interpreted
  path but the bake is *identical* for the IR path — fold it in here rather
  than treating textures as a later sprint.
- Mario → the quad-optimized mesh IR that already animates in
  `marioturntable`, bridged to the **live** animation state instead of a
  canned frame.

### Step 2 — bridge the live game state

Sourceboot's simulation (untouched, authoritative) feeds the
castleviewer-class renderer: Mario position/action/animation frame, camera,
and initially **nothing else**. Dynamic objects absent or deferred; the
cannon can come early since its IR is trivial. The ownership contract in
`ENGINE_PORT_ARCHITECTURE.md` is unchanged — this is precisely the
"renderer consumes, never owns" split it mandates.

### Step 3 — degrade like Croc shipped

View distance first (the owner's explicit lever, and Croc's), then LOD tiers
from the texture spec's near/mid/far variants. Fill rate is ~6% of the frame,
so viewport shrinking stays off the list — degrade geometry and distance, not
pixels.

## What survives from the parity sprint

The 12-gate sprint (`2026-07-27-saturn-native-ps1-parity-sprint.md`) becomes
the **correctness ratchet behind the demo path, not the schedule in front of
it.**

- **Task 0's frozen route and comparator: keep, and keep using.** The
  checkpoint hash covers *simulation* state (Mario position bits, action,
  `gGlobalTimer`) — exactly what must not drift while the renderer is
  swapped. Render counters will change meaning under the IR path: bump the
  route schema version per its own extension rule rather than comparing
  incompatible fields.
- **Task 1's Q16/DIVU kernels: directly reused** — they are the IR path's
  math.
- **Task 2's differential oracle: keep the gate, absorb the trace.** The
  Q16-vs-float gate (`fac4a0f`) remains the correctness net for any runtime
  transform the bridge performs. The stalled trace-capture work is
  deprioritized — the IR path replaces the code it was meant to instrument.
- Tasks 3–9 fold into the demo path where they overlap (packets ≈ the IR
  itself; culling/order = step 3; slave SH-2 and audio unchanged, later).
- **The 15 FPS median / 12 FPS 1%-low exit gate stands.** So does the rule:
  if it misses, the work stays open — no relabeling.

## Standing constraints (unchanged, restated because the pace is changing)

- The simulation remains authoritative. The renderer may cache, lower, and
  degrade; it may not own game state.
- Licence discipline: Z-Treme and SlaveDriver are GPL-adoptable isolated in
  `src/port/saturn/gpl/`; Jo Engine BSD (notice retained); `sm64-psx`
  behaviour-only (no licence). Ledger in the sprint plan §4; provenance in
  `PROVENANCE.md`.
- Evidence discipline: frozen-route captures, append-only profile fields,
  `fast3d_profile_decode.py`, fresh symbol resolution every build, CUE mtime
  newer than ELF (the stale-CUE trap has cost real time twice), 1500 s+
  capture timeouts, no capture concurrent with a build.
- **The user's eyes remain the acceptance gate** for anything visual — and
  the demo path exists to give them something worth gating.

## Known risks, stated up front

1. **Sim-tick cost is unmeasured** (Step 0 exists to kill this unknown).
2. **The bridge is where correctness bugs will live** — live animation state
   into the mesh IR is the least-proven seam. The differential gate and the
   route checkpoint are the nets.
3. **Coverage debt is deliberate**: no dynamic objects at first. That is the
   accepted cost of seeing terrain, Mario, and textures at speed; the sprint
   gates pull coverage back in afterwards.
4. Croc is a purpose-built engine with grid terrain and small rooms — the
   comparison justifies the *target*, not every detail of the path. Where BOB
   differs (open sightlines), view distance is the equalizer, as the owner
   said.
